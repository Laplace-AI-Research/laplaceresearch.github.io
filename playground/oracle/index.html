How the odds work
  1. StartOn and off are equally likely: odds 1 : 1, or 50%.
  2. A positiveA positive is four times as likely when the signal is on (0.8 ÷ 0.2 = 4). Odds 4 : 1, or 4/5 = 80%.
  3. Same sensorOnce A reads positive, its copy is certain to read positive whether the signal is on or off. Its likelihood ratio is 1, so the odds stay 4 : 1, or 80%.
  4. IndependentA second sensor errs independently, given the signal. Its positive multiplies the odds by 4 again: 16 : 1, or 16/17 = 94.1%.

Where the model numbers come from

Aquila’s own episode read A at a 50% prior, saw a positive, and stopped at 80%. The two-reading numbers come from separate fixed-history tests run in November 2026, in which the evaluator supplied the readings: A positive, then A and B positive, under each wiring. In the same-sensor test the models had been told explicitly that B copies A. Every value is a single recorded response from a named checkpoint, so it describes this positive case, not how often a model behaves this way.

No model runs in your browser. The page replays stored numbers, and your answer stays in this page’s memory.

The experiment in brief

The interactive version needs JavaScript. Here is what it shows.

A hidden signal is on or off, equally likely. Sensor A reads positive 80% of the time when the signal is on and 20% when it is off. Two displays are wired to sensor A, and each costs two of your four credits to read. In the recorded case, display A reads positive: the exact forecast is 80%, and Oracle Aquila also said 80%, then stopped with two credits left.

A copy of A adds no evidence, so the exact answer stays at 80%. An independent sensor multiplies the odds by four again, to 16/17. Aquila’s recorded answer was 90.5% under both wirings. It made the right purchasing decision but misjudged how much the copy told it.
