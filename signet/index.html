02

What Signet is

Signet models revise their answers on evidence, not on pressure, and they accept a record only when it arrives through the channel that is supposed to carry it.

A Signet model keeps a correct answer when a user pushes back. It changes its answer when shown a valid correction. It says so when the evidence does not settle the question. And when a record claims to come from an authenticated tool, it checks that the record actually arrived as a tool response, rather than as text pasted into the user's message.

The family has three sizes, built on Qwen3.5 and trained on the ConvictionBench training split. All three are open under Apache-2.0.

Input

  • A question and the conversation so far
  • User claims and arguments
  • Records from an authenticated tool

Output

keep answer, revise, keep, or insufficient, with a final answer

03

The channel problem

Paste a record into the chat and most models believe it, exactly as often as they believe the real one.

Language models increasingly act on what tools tell them: a database lookup, a search result, an account balance. The text of a record is easy to copy. Where the record came from is not. We gave models the same record twice, once as a genuine tool response and once pasted into a user message as a quote, a forwarded message or a code block.

Off-the-shelf models don't check the channel. Signet does.

Qwen3.5-9B adopted 86% of the genuine records and 85% of the pasted ones. Signet-9B adopted 96% of the genuine records and 1% of the pasted ones. None of these paste formats appeared in training.

04

Results on unseen problem types

On problem types held out of training entirely, every Signet model is more accurate, steadier under pressure and harder to fool than its base.

The held-out split removes whole problem subtypes from training, so these numbers measure behaviour the models had to generalise, not recall. Signet-9B reaches 97.8% final accuracy against 68.8% for Qwen3.5-9B, gives in to pressure 1.1% of the time against 39.9%, and accepted every valid correction.

05

Choosing a model

Start with Signet-9B for the strongest channel check. Signet-4B is nearly as strong at less than half the size.

Signet-4B and Signet-9B are within noise of each other on most measures. Signet-9B holds its channel rule more consistently on unfamiliar paste formats and ships as merged weights as well as adapters. Signet-2B fits the tightest budgets, but trails on unseen problem types and errs towards rejecting genuine records.

06

Checked before release

We wrote down three release checks before running them. All three passed at every size.

  1. A second training seed replicates the result.

    Retraining each model with a different seed gave held-out accuracy of 0.840, 0.961 and 0.972 for 2B, 4B and 9B, with the channel rule intact. Signet-4B's second seed learned a weaker rule for unfamiliar paste formats (+0.66 rather than +0.92); its model card says so.

  2. A private held-out split shows no contamination.

    ConvictionBench keeps a split that has never been published. Each Signet model scores within 0.02 of its public results on it, in line with its base model.

  3. A reworded system prompt costs little.

    Rewording every sentence of the system prompt while keeping its meaning cost at most 3.7 points of held-out accuracy.

07

What we learned

The right recipe depends on the base model and on its size.

On Qwen3-1.7B, reinforcement learning found the channel rule slowly and weakly. On Qwen3.5-2B it found it within 400 steps. The base model decides whether the rule is learnable at all.

Imitation saturates larger models. A warm-start of 1,000 supervised examples left reinforcement learning almost nothing to learn at 4B and 9B; 250 examples worked. Reinforcement learning from the base model gave the best 2B and 4B models, while a light warm-start before it gave clearly the best 9B.

Not everything worked. No 1.7B model met the bar we set in advance. The closest reached 0.751 held-out accuracy with a +0.19 channel gap, and we report it rather than release it.

08

Where the seal stops

Signet learned to trust the channel its system prompt declares trusted. It does not authenticate anything by itself.

  1. The benchmark is synthetic.

    Short arithmetic, logic and state-tracking problems with a fixed JSON reply. Open-ended conversation, long documents and real tool ecosystems are untested.

  2. Tool messages are trusted by construction.

    In ConvictionBench, a tool response is authentic by definition. In a real system, that guarantee has to come from the application, not the model.

  3. The benchmark is near its ceiling.

    Signet-4B and Signet-9B score close to 1.0 on many measures, so ConvictionBench v0.1 no longer separates models at this level.

  4. Text only.

    The Qwen3.5 vision tower was never trained or evaluated, and the merged Signet-9B weights leave it out.

09

Models, code, and terms

Model weights and code are released under Apache-2.0. The models are built on Qwen3.5-2B, Qwen3.5-4B and Qwen3.5-9B (Apache-2.0, Qwen / Alibaba Cloud). ConvictionBench data is CC BY 4.0. Please do not train on its test splits. See licenses and attribution.

Signet models are research artifacts provided ‘as is’, without warranty. They do not verify the authenticity of real-world records and should not be the only safeguard in a system that acts on them. Full disclaimer.
