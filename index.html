Benchmark · 19 Nov 2026

ConvictionBench: evidence-sensitive belief revision in language models

When challenged, a language model has to decide whether to keep its answer, revise it, or say the evidence is insufficient. ConvictionBench measures how that decision moves with the quality of the evidence and the pressure to agree, across 26,478 generated episodes.

Arya Somu, Bruce Nshuti Hirwa

How we work

About the lab →

  1. Calibration before confidence

    A model’s stated probability should match how often it is right. We measure that first, and treat a sharper answer as worth little until its confidence can be trusted.

  2. Claims travel with evidence

    Releases link their model artifacts, evaluation inputs, metric code, limitations, and reproduction instructions. Where a result is not statistically resolved, we say so.

  3. Data and mechanisms together

    We aggregate resolved forecasting data across markets, history, and expert judgment, and study the mechanisms that produce calibrated belief. Each tells us where to look next.
