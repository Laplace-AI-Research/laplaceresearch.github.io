01

Laplace Research builds foundation models for calibrated forecasting.

A forecast is only useful if you know how much to trust it. Models now produce predictions at scale, and almost none of them arrive with confidence you can take at face value. We work on closing that gap: aggregating resolved forecasting data across markets, history, and expert judgment, and studying the mechanisms that produce calibrated belief.

Every release ships with its model artifacts, evaluation inputs, metric code, and limitations, so the claims can be checked. Read our latest release.

03

Introducing ConvictionBench

When challenged, a language model has to decide whether to keep its answer, revise it, or say the evidence is insufficient. ConvictionBench measures how that decision moves with the quality of the evidence and the pressure to agree, across 26,478 generated episodes in arithmetic, ledgers, logic, and small rule-based worlds.

We released it with the Laplace Conviction Series, three Qwen3-1.7B adapters that each train for belief revision a different way. Read the release.

    05

    How we work

    Two problems tighten each other. Better data sharpens calibration, and understanding why a model is calibrated tells us which data to collect next.

    1. i

      Calibration before confidence

      A stated probability should match how often the model is right. We measure that first.

    2. ii

      Claims travel with evidence

      Releases link artifacts, evaluation inputs, metric code, and limitations. Unresolved results are labelled as such.

    3. iii

      Open by default

      Weights, evaluation data, and code are public, so the headline numbers can be recomputed.
