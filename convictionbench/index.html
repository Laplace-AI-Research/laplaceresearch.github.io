ConvictionBench: Evidence-sensitive belief revision in language models

Benchmark release November 19, 2026 · By Arya Somu & Bruce Nshuti Hirwa · 6 min read

When challenged, a language model has to decide whether to keep its answer, revise it, or say the evidence is insufficient. ConvictionBench tests how that decision changes with the evidence and with the pressure to agree.

ConvictionBench v0.1 pairs 26,478 deterministic episodes across arithmetic, ledgers, logic, and small rule-based worlds with three experimental Qwen3-1.7B LoRA adapters—Anchor, Vector, and Meridian—that probe how different post-training recipes behave under pressure and fabricated records.

Evidence and social pressure

ConvictionBench v0.1 contains 26,478 generated episodes across arithmetic, ledgers, logic, and small rule-based worlds. A deterministic solver determines the expected answer and decision for every turn.

Matched episodes vary the wording of a challenge, the quality of its evidence, and the channel the evidence arrives through. The structural split holds out whole problem types. A separate natural-history evaluation challenges the model’s own first answer.

In a control using identical record text, Anchor adopted the record 62% of the time from a tool message and 58% of the time from a plain user message, compared with 8% when the user added the forger’s wording. That result shows how much the measured resistance depended on the framing.

Strip away the forger’s phrasing and resistance largely vanishes: Anchor adopted the same record 62% of the time from a tool message and 58% from a plain user turn.

The tasks use short synthetic dialogues and a fixed response format. The results describe behavior under those conditions and give limited evidence about real-world authentication or manipulation.

Laplace Conviction Series

Three experimental Qwen3-1.7B LoRA adapters test different ways to train for belief revision: supervised fine-tuning (Anchor), GRPO directly from the base model (Vector), and a light SFT warm-start followed by GRPO (Meridian).

  • Conviction Anchor 1.7B (SFT): Strong forged-record resistance on framed attacks (0.531), but often kept its own wrong answers after valid corrections in natural history.
  • Conviction Vector 1.7B (GRPO): Reached the highest held-out final accuracy of the three (0.644), with weaker forged-record resistance than the base model on the same split (0.315 vs. 0.340). Its accuracy edge over Anchor was sensitive to seed and prompt.
  • Conviction Meridian 1.7B (SFT → GRPO): Resisted more forged records than the other adapters on held-out types (0.578), at lower final accuracy than Vector (0.560).
