01

Safety & Responsibility

Understanding the future demands responsibility.

Laplace Research develops intelligence systems to help humanity understand an uncertain world. We believe that the pursuit of increasingly capable forecasting and temporal reasoning technology must be accompanied by scientific rigour, transparency and responsibility.

Our approach is grounded in the conviction that intelligence systems should expand humanity’s capacity to understand consequential events while preserving human agency, accountability and dignity. This page sets out the principles we work by, how we evaluate and release models, and where our practices are still being built. It is a living document, and it will change as our research does.

02

Our commitments

Six principles govern how we conduct research and how we release it. They are the standard we hold our own work to, and the reference point for every policy on this page.

  1. 01

    Epistemic integrity

    Our systems should communicate what they know, what they infer and what they cannot reliably determine. We recognise the distinction between verified knowledge, probabilistic inference and speculation.

  2. 02

    Calibrated uncertainty

    Forecasts should communicate uncertainty rather than present possible futures as inevitable outcomes. Probability calibration and accurate representations of uncertainty are central to reliable forecasting.

  3. 03

    Human agency

    Our technology should inform human judgement rather than eliminate human accountability in consequential decisions.

  4. 04

    Responsible deployment

    The accessibility and deployment of our technology should reflect its demonstrated capabilities, limitations and potential for misuse.

  5. 05

    Scientific transparency

    We aim to communicate research methods, evaluation results, limitations and negative findings wherever responsibly possible.

  6. 06

    Societal resilience

    Our research should contribute to a safer, better-informed and better-prepared society.

03

Forecasting integrity

A forecast is a claim about what will happen, offered with a stated degree of confidence. People act on that confidence. A system that is accurate on average but overconfident, quietly informed by hindsight, or easy to steer with planted information can mislead the very people it is meant to help.

For this reason we treat forecasting integrity as a safety property, not only a performance metric. The risks below are the ones we consider most specific to forecasting and temporal reasoning. Each is marked with how far our current work addresses it.

Integrity risks in forecasting systems
RiskWhat can go wrongResearch and mitigation directionStatus
OverconfidenceStated probabilities run ahead of how often events occur, so forecasts are trusted more than they should be.Proper scoring rules, calibration measurement and explicit communication of uncertainty.Current practice
Fabricated evidenceA model invents evidence, or accepts records that are forged or cannot be traced.Evidence provenance, source grounding and factual reliability.Current practice
Temporal leakageTraining or evaluation data contains information that was unavailable at the forecast cutoff, inflating measured skill.Time-indexed evaluation protocols that withhold everything after the cutoff.Proposed
Forecast manipulationMisleading, adversarial or deliberately planted information shifts a forecast.Robustness evaluation against misleading inputs and social pressure.Current practice
Self-fulfilling predictionsPublishing a forecast changes the behaviour of the people it describes, and so the outcome.Study of how disseminating forecasts can influence the events being predicted.Open question
Historical fabricationAn inferred reconstruction of the past is presented as established fact.Clear separation of the verified historical record from inferred reconstruction.Proposed
Distribution shiftReliability measured in one setting does not hold in an unfamiliar one.Evaluation on held-out problem types and unseen formats; re-measurement when the domain or horizon changes.Current practice

‘Current practice’ means at least one published Laplace release measures or documents the risk. It does not mean every model has been evaluated against it.

Methods

We separate the methods our published work already uses from those we intend to adopt. Each release page states which evaluations it reports.

Used in published work

  • Brier score, decomposed into calibration and resolution, and calibration error — Tarot-Draw.
  • Time-indexed inputs: every Tarot-Draw question carries an explicit as_of date.
  • Contamination check against a never-published held-out split whose hash alone is public — Signet.
  • Out-of-distribution testing on held-out problem subtypes and unseen wordings — ConvictionBench.
  • Evidence provenance: whether a model adopts an identical record arriving through a trusted or an untrusted channel — Signet.
  • Abstention: ConvictionBench scores whether a model says the evidence is insufficient.

Research directions

  • Logarithmic scoring reported alongside the Brier score.
  • Reliability diagrams published with forecasting releases.
  • Contamination-resistant protocols for forecasting corpora, scoring only questions that resolve after a model’s training cutoff.
  • Evidence attribution for forecasts that draw on retrieved sources.
  • Uncertainty estimation beyond a single probability, including abstention for forecasts.

04

Model evaluation and release

Proposed framework

We are developing a framework for deciding how, and whether, to release a model. It is a proposal, not yet formal policy, and it will become more rigorous as Laplace grows. Our practice today is narrower: every release so far has been an open release of weights, evaluation data and code, and each release page reports the evaluations we ran, the limitations we found and the scripts that reproduce its headline numbers.

Four evaluation dimensions

  • Capability

    • Forecasting performance
    • Probabilistic reasoning
    • Domain generalisation
    • Temporal reasoning
    • Evidence synthesis

  • Reliability

    • Calibration
    • Robustness
    • Consistency
    • Uncertainty communication
    • Performance under distribution shift

  • Safety

    • Potential misuse
    • Dual-use implications
    • Harmful or misleading outputs
    • Adversarial robustness
    • Risks in consequential decision-making

  • Data integrity

    • Training data provenance
    • Evaluation contamination
    • Temporal leakage
    • Dataset documentation
    • Reproducibility

Release decisions

Open release
Public access to model weights, research artifacts or inference.
Controlled release
Restricted or staged access when identified risks warrant it.
Deferred release
Further evaluation or mitigation before any public deployment.

Laplace does not have a dedicated safety team, an external review board or comprehensive evaluation infrastructure. Under this proposal, release reviews would be conducted and signed off by the founders, who would seek outside expertise where a risk exceeds our own.

05

National security and dual-use research

Laplace Research recognises that advances in forecasting, temporal reasoning and world simulation may have significant implications for national and international security.

We believe these technologies can contribute to conflict prevention, humanitarian preparedness, critical infrastructure resilience, economic security, disaster response and strategic understanding. National security is one potential application of a broader scientific mission, not the mission itself.

We oppose the development and deployment of our technology for autonomous lethal targeting, unlawful surveillance, political repression and other applications that undermine fundamental human rights.

As our systems become more capable, we intend to evaluate their dual-use potential and adopt safeguards proportionate to the risks they present. Our objective is to advance humanity’s capacity to anticipate and understand consequential events while preserving human agency, accountability and dignity.

Potential beneficial applications

  • Humanitarian preparedness
  • Geopolitical risk assessment
  • Conflict prevention and de-escalation
  • Critical infrastructure resilience
  • Economic security
  • Disaster preparedness and response
  • Supply chain resilience
  • Historical reconstruction for intelligence analysis

06

Responsible scaling

Proposed framework

The Laplace Responsible Scaling Framework (LRSF) is a proposal for how evaluation, oversight and deployment safeguards should grow as our systems become more capable. It describes four illustrative capability levels. Safeguards are cumulative: each level keeps those of the levels below it.

  1. Level 1

    Foundational forecasting

    Domain-specific forecasting, probability estimation and benchmark-oriented reasoning.

    Proposed safeguards

    • Standard capability and reliability evaluations
    • Model documentation
    • Disclosure of known limitations

  2. Level 2

    Generalised forecasting

    Cross-domain forecasting, evidence synthesis and complex probabilistic reasoning.

    Proposed safeguards

    • Expanded robustness evaluations
    • Cross-domain calibration assessment
    • Misuse evaluations
    • Documented release review

  3. Level 3

    Advanced temporal intelligence

    Complex world simulation, counterfactual reasoning and advanced reconstruction of historical states.

    Proposed safeguards

    • Dedicated dual-use assessments
    • More comprehensive adversarial evaluation
    • Deployment safeguards where warranted
    • External expert review where feasible

  4. Level 4

    Strategically significant intelligence

    Demonstrated capabilities that could materially affect consequential institutional decisions, societal systems or national security.

    Proposed safeguards

    • Formal risk assessments
    • Heightened governance and oversight
    • External consultation where appropriate
    • Potentially controlled or deferred deployment
    • Ongoing monitoring where technically and operationally feasible

These levels are conceptual, not validated capability thresholds. The measurable thresholds, evaluation protocols and governance procedures for each level are still being developed. We have not assigned any current Laplace model to a level, and will not do so without supporting evaluations.

Open questions

  • Measuring the thresholds

    Which evaluations would show that a system has crossed from one level to the next, and with what confidence.

    Open question

  • Evaluating simulation and counterfactuals

    How to score counterfactual and historical reasoning when there is no resolved outcome to compare against.

    Open question

  • Reflexive effects

    How to measure the influence a widely read forecast has on the outcome it describes.

    Open question

07

Research integrity

Forecasting research is unusually easy to fool yourself with: hindsight leaks into data, benchmarks saturate, and small evaluation sets reward noise. We hold our published work to the standards below. In time this section may become a standalone research integrity policy.

  • Honest reporting of results

    We publish results that do not favour our models, including seed-sensitive and negative findings, such as a Conviction Series adapter that resisted fewer forged records than its base model.

    Current practice

  • Transparent benchmark methods

    Splits, scoring rules and oracles are published with each benchmark, as in the ConvictionBench paper.

    Current practice

  • Reproducibility

    Release pages link the evaluation inputs and scripts needed to recompute their headline numbers.

    Current practice

  • Attribution of prior work

    Base models, datasets and their licences are credited on Licenses and on each release.

    Current practice

  • Dataset provenance

    We document where training and evaluation data come from, including data we cannot redistribute.

    Current practice

  • Documented limitations

    Each release states where its results stop holding.

    Current practice

  • Temporal contamination prevention

    Protocols that guarantee forecasting evaluations use only information available before each question’s cutoff.

    Proposed

  • Findings, hypotheses and speculation

    We label which claims are measured, which are hypotheses and which are research directions, as this page does.

    Commitment

  • Corrections

    If we find an error in published work, we will correct the original page, note the date and nature of the change, and list it under Transparency and publications.

    Commitment

09

Governance and accountability

Laplace Research is a small, founder-led research organisation. We would rather describe modest processes accurately than claim governance infrastructure we do not have. Responsibility for research, release and this document rests with the founders.

  • Documented release decisions

    Record the evidence and reasoning behind each consequential model release.

    Proposed

  • Named responsibility

    Assign a named person to each evaluation and deployment decision.

    Proposed

  • Review of safety concerns

    Review every safety concern that is reported to us or identified internally, and record the outcome.

    Proposed

  • Policies that keep pace

    Update this document as our research capabilities change, and record each revision below.

    Commitment

  • Outside expertise

    Seek external expertise when a risk exceeds what we can assess ourselves.

    Commitment

10

Reporting a concern

If a Laplace model, dataset or publication behaves in a way that could cause harm, or you find a flaw in our research, please tell us. Include the model or dataset and its version, what you did, what happened and why it concerns you.

  • Model safety concerns

    Harmful, misleading or unsafe model behaviour.

    safety@laplaceresearch.org

  • Potential misuse

    Uses of our models or data that could cause harm.

    safety@laplaceresearch.org

  • Significant reliability failures

    Miscalibrated, inconsistent or badly wrong outputs.

    safety@laplaceresearch.org

  • Research integrity concerns

    Errors, contamination or unsupported claims in our published work.

    safety@laplaceresearch.org

  • Security vulnerabilities

    Vulnerabilities in our code, models or this site. Please report privately rather than in a public issue.

    inquiries@laplaceresearch.org

  • Privacy concerns

    Personal data in our models, datasets or site. See also the privacy notice.

    inquiries@laplaceresearch.org

Please send only the personal information needed to describe the issue. We do not currently run a bug bounty or offer guaranteed response times.
