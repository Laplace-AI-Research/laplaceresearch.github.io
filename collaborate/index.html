01

Collaborations

Advancing the science of forecasting, together.

Progress in forecasting and temporal intelligence needs contributions from researchers, engineers, scientific institutions and infrastructure partners across many disciplines.

Laplace Research welcomes collaboration on fundamental research, open-source development, scientific infrastructure and responsible applications of forecasting. We are most interested in work that adds to rigorous scientific understanding and reproducible research, and that has a clear benefit to society.

We are a small, founder-led research organisation, and this page is written accordingly. It describes the kinds of collaboration we are open to and how to start a conversation. It does not describe existing partnerships, programmes or open positions, because we do not yet have any to announce.

Ways to collaborate

Six kinds of collaboration fit our work. Each one connects to the scientific questions in our research agenda; a proposal that does not connect to them is unlikely to be a good fit, however interesting it is.

01

Academic and scientific research

We welcome collaboration with researchers, university laboratories and independent research groups working on related scientific problems. Formats might include joint research projects, shared experimental methods, independent evaluations of our models and co-authored publications.

Areas

  • Probabilistic reasoning
  • Forecasting and calibration
  • Causal inference
  • Temporal reasoning
  • World models
  • Reinforcement learning
  • Evaluation methodology
  • Epistemic intelligence

02

Open-source contributions

Our public code, models and data are open to contributions, and no introduction is needed to start. Useful contributions include reproduction experiments, independent analysis, bug reports and fixes, documentation, model integrations, improvements to datasets and new benchmark implementations.

Public repositories

  • conviction — ConvictionBench evaluation code, data and results (Apache-2.0).
  • signet — Signet training code, configurations, results and pre-registration log (Apache-2.0).
  • ConvictionBench dataset — the benchmark episodes on Hugging Face (CC BY 4.0).
  • Models — every open checkpoint is listed in the model directory.

Our repositories do not yet publish contribution guidelines. For anything larger than a small fix, please open an issue describing the change before sending a pull request. All organisations: GitHub and Hugging Face.

03

Compute and research infrastructure

Training and evaluating forecasting models takes substantial compute. We are interested in working with organisations that can support specific research objectives, such as a training run, an evaluation campaign or a reproducible experimental environment, rather than general sponsorship.

Areas

  • GPU compute
  • Model training infrastructure
  • Inference infrastructure
  • Research experimentation platforms
  • Distributed training
  • Data processing
  • Evaluation infrastructure

04

Data and evaluation partnerships

Forecasting research depends on well-specified questions, reliably resolved outcomes and data whose timing is known. We welcome researchers and organisations with relevant data, domain expertise or evaluation capacity.

Areas

  • Historical datasets
  • Temporally indexed information
  • Forecasting questions and outcomes
  • Scientific datasets
  • Benchmark design
  • Dataset provenance
  • Independent evaluation
  • Domain-specific forecasting expertise

We work only with data that has been acquired lawfully, with the permissions and licences needed for research use, and with appropriate protection for privacy. Please do not offer personal data, improperly obtained datasets or confidential institutional records.

05

Institutional and applied research

We are open to exploratory research with organisations studying responsible uses of forecasting. We are a research organisation, not a software vendor or contractor, and collaborations of this kind begin as joint research.

Domains

  • Scientific discovery
  • Economic and financial research
  • Critical infrastructure resilience
  • Public policy analysis
  • Humanitarian preparedness
  • Environmental and climate risk
  • Disaster preparedness
  • Supply chain resilience
  • Conflict prevention and national security

Every collaboration must be consistent with our Safety & Responsibility principles, including the limits set out in National security and dual-use research.

06

Research funding and grants

We welcome inquiries from foundations, research funders, grant programmes and institutions interested in supporting fundamental research in forecasting.

Areas of support

  • Research compute
  • Dataset development
  • Open-source scientific infrastructure
  • Independent evaluations
  • Fundamental forecasting research
  • Research fellowships, where feasible
  • Scientific dissemination

This is an invitation to discuss research support. It is not an offer of investment or securities, and this site is not a channel for investment inquiries.

03

What we look for

These are the principles we use to judge whether a collaboration is likely to work well for both sides. They are a guide to the conversation, not a checklist or an application process.

  1. 01

    Scientific alignment

    The work should contribute meaningfully to the questions in our research agenda.

  2. 02

    Intellectual rigour

    Collaborators should value careful experiments, transparent reasoning and conclusions that follow from evidence.

  3. 03

    Research integrity

    The work should respect data provenance, proper attribution and reproducibility.

  4. 04

    Responsible application

    Proposed work should be consistent with our safety principles and ethical limits.

  5. 05

    Mutual contribution

    Objectives, responsibilities and the expected scientific benefit should be clear for both sides.

  6. 06

    Independence

    Both sides should be able to report findings honestly, subject to agreed confidentiality and publication terms.

04

How collaboration works

Most collaborations follow the same four steps. The process is deliberately simple, because we are a small team.

We read every introduction, but we cannot promise to take on every proposal or to reply within a fixed time. Sending an inquiry does not create a research agreement or any obligation on either side.

05

Individual researchers and engineers

Individual researchers and engineers who want to contribute to our scientific mission are welcome to introduce themselves. This is not a careers page, and it helps to be clear about which of these you are asking about.

  • Open-source contributions

    Issues and pull requests to our public repositories. Anyone can contribute.

    Open to all

  • Independent research collaboration

    Joint work with an individual researcher on a question in our agenda, agreed case by case.

    By introduction

  • Formal research partnerships

    Institutional collaborations with a written scope and terms, as described in How collaboration works.

    By agreement

  • Employment

    Paid positions, internships and fellowships. None are open at present; any that open will be announced on this site.

    None open

06

Make an introduction

Collaboration inquiries go to inquiries@laplaceresearch.org, which the founders read. Choosing the closest category below opens an email with a subject line that helps us route it.

  • Academic research

    Joint research, shared methods, evaluations, co-authorship.

    Email us

  • Open-source contributions

    Larger contributions you would like to discuss first.

    Email us

  • Compute and infrastructure

    Compute or infrastructure for specific research objectives.

    Email us

  • Data and evaluations

    Datasets, forecasting questions, benchmark design, independent evaluation.

    Email us

  • Institutional research

    Exploratory research on responsible applications.

    Email us

  • Research funding and grants

    Support for fundamental research.

    Email us

  • Other

    Anything that connects to our research agenda but fits none of the above.

    Email us

What to include

  • Required: your name, the collaboration category and a short description of what you propose and how it connects to our research agenda.
  • Optional: your institution or organisation, your role or research affiliation, a website, GitHub, Google Scholar or other professional profile, and links to relevant publications or projects.

Please do not send confidential, sensitive or proprietary information, or attachments, in a first message. If a collaboration goes ahead, we will agree how to share such material. We use your email address and message only to reply, as described in our privacy notice. To report a safety concern instead, see Reporting a concern.

07

Current partnerships

Laplace Research has no formal research partnerships, sponsors or institutional affiliations to announce. When a partnership is agreed and both sides have approved its announcement, it will be listed here with a short description of the work. We do not list organisations on the basis of individual contributors’ personal affiliations.

08

Work with us.

We believe meaningful progress in forecasting and temporal intelligence will come from rigorous research, diverse perspectives and sustained scientific collaboration.

If your work intersects with our research agenda, we welcome an introduction.
